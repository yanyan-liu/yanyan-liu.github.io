Inverse Problems¶

The goal of unmixing is to recover the vector \(\mathbf{c}\) of unknown concentration maps from acoustic measurements \(\mathbf{p}\) via the following linear system

(82)¶\[ \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}\mathbf{S} \mathbf{c} + \mathbf{n},\]

where \(\mathbf{n}\) represents measurement noise.

Two-step Approach¶

In the two-step approach, one states a minimization problem to find solution \(\bar{\mathbf{b}}\) to the acoustic problem in Eq. (80)

(83)¶\[ \overline{\mathbf{b}} \in \arg\min_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{NL}} \left\{\frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}} \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{p}\|^2_2 + \mathcal{R}_1(\mathbf{b}) + \delta_{\geq 0}(\mathbf{b}) \right\},\]

where the indicator function \(\delta_{\geq 0}(\mathbf{x})\) enforces nonnegativity and is defined for a vector \(\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^N\) as

(84)¶\[\begin{split} \delta_{\geq0}(\mathbf{x}) = \left\{ \begin{aligned} &0, \quad \text{if } x_n \geq 0, \quad n=1, \ldots, N\\ &+\infty, \quad \text{else}. \end{aligned} \right.\end{split}\]

One then solves the unmixing problem by determining

(85)¶\[ \overline{\mathbf{c}} \in \arg\min_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{PL}} \left\{\frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{S} \mathbf{c} - \overline{\mathbf{b}}\|^2_2 + \mathcal{R}_2 (\mathbf{c}) + \delta_{\geq 0}(\mathbf{c})\right\}.\]

The regularization terms \(\mathcal{R}_1(\cdot)\) and \(\mathcal{R}_2(\cdot)\) are optional. Here we chose to apply total-variation (TV) on \(\mathbf{b}\) for smoothness in the reconstructed images and a combination of \(\ell_1\) and TV on \(\mathbf{c}\) which was shown effective in the precious part:

(86)¶\[ \mathcal{R}_1(\mathbf{b}) = \nu_1 \left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|\mathbf{b}^n|_\text{TV}\right), \]
(87)¶\[ \mathcal{R}_2(\mathbf{c}) = \nu_2 \left( \sum_{i_1=0}^{L_{\text{x}} - 1} \sum_{i_2=0}^{L_{\text{z}} - 1} \|\mathbf{c}_{i_1, i_2}\|_1 \right) + \nu_3 \left(\sum_{p=1}^{P}|\mathbf{c}^p|_\text{TV}\right), \]

\(\nu_1, \nu_2\) and \(\nu_3\) are nonnegative regularization weights. We adopt anisotropic TV for computational speed. It enforces sparsity in the gradient domain and is defined as

(88)¶\[ |\cdot|_{\text{TV}} = \|\nabla_\text{x} (\cdot)\|_1 + \|\nabla_\text{z} (\cdot)\|_1.\]

where \(\nabla_\text{x}\) and \(\nabla_\text{z}\) are the finite difference operators in lateral and axial directions, respectively.

One-step Approach¶

We formulate the outcome of the one-step inversion as solution \(\hat{\mathbf{c}}\) to the minimization problem

(89)¶\[ \hat{\mathbf{c}} \in \arg\min_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{PL}} \left\{\frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{A} \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{p}\|^2_2 + \mathcal{R}_3(\mathbf{c}) +\delta_{\geq0}(\mathbf{c}) \right\},\]

where \(\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}\mathbf{S}\) is the composite forward operator, \(\mathcal{R}_3(\mathbf{c})\) is a regularization term defined as

(90)¶\[ \mathcal{R}_3(\mathbf{c}) = \lambda_1 \left(\sum_{p=1}^P |\mathbf{c}^{p}|_\text{TV}\right) + \lambda_2 \left(\sum_{i_1=0}^{L_{\text{x}} - 1} \sum_{i_2=0}^{L_{\text{z}} - 1} \|\mathbf{c}_{i_1, i_2}\|_1 \right) + \lambda_3 \left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{c})^n|_{\text{TV}}\right), \]

and nonnegative constants \(\lambda_1\), \(\lambda_2\) and \(\lambda_3\) are the respective regularization weights. The first term in Eq. (90) applies TV to the spatial concentration maps \(\mathbf{c}^p\in\mathbb{R}^{L}\), \(p=1, \ldots, P\) of all \(P\) species to reduce noise and achieve a smooth reconstruction. The second term in Eq. (90) applies sparsity-promoting \(\ell_1\)-norm to each pixel in the concentration map \(\mathbf{c}_{i_1, i_2}\in\mathbb{R}^P\), \(i_1=0, \ldots, (L_{\text{x}} - 1), i_2=0, \ldots, (L_{\text{z}}-1)\), in order to minimize cross-talk between different species. The last regularizer in Eq. (90) applies TV to spatial intensity maps of PA images \(\mathbf{H}^n=(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{c})^n\in\mathbb{R}^{L}\), \(n=0, \ldots, (N-1)\) of all \(N\) switching pulses to enforce a smoothing effect on the intermediate PA images.

Algorithm¶

The objective functions of the minimization problems in Eq. (83), Eq. (85), and Eq. (89) share a similar structure of a smooth part (quadratic data-fidelity term), which we denote by \(f\), and a nonsmooth part (sparsity-based regularization term and nonnegativity constraint), which we denote by \(w\). Further, the three regularizers introduced in Eq. (86), Eq. (87), and Eq. (90) can be rewritten in the form of the \(L_1\)-norm of an operator \(\mathbf{L}\) as \(\mathcal{R}(\cdot) = \|\mathbf{L}(\cdot)\|_1\). For example, \(\mathbf{L}\) for \(\mathcal{R}_3\) is

(91)¶\[\begin{split} \mathbf{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{L}_1 \\ \mathbf{L}_2 \\ \mathbf{L}_3 \end{bmatrix},\end{split}\]

where

(92)¶\[\begin{split} \mathbf{L}_1 = \lambda_1 \begin{bmatrix} \nabla_\text{x}\\ \nabla_\text{z} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{L}_2 = \lambda_2 \begin{bmatrix} \nabla_\text{x} \cdot \mathbf{S}\\ \nabla_\text{z} \cdot \mathbf{S} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{L}_3 = \lambda_3 \mathbf{I},\end{split}\]

and \(\mathbf{I}\) is the identity operator. The operators \(\mathbf{L}\) of \(\mathcal{R}_1\) and \(\mathcal{R}_2\) are similar and thus omitted here in the interest of space.

We use Eq. (89) as an example to show how to obtain the solution, as Eq. (83) and Eq. (85) follow the same approach. The objective function in Eq. (89) is composed of a smooth part \(f(\cdot)=\frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{A} \cdot - \mathbf{p}\|^2_2\) and a nonsmooth part \(w(\cdot)=\mathcal{R}_3(\cdot) + \delta_{\geq0}(\cdot)\). We thus deploy a proximal-gradient method combined with the fast iterative shrinkage thresholding algorithm (FISTA) [77] to obtain the solution. Detailed steps are presented in Algorithm 3. There, the key is the computation of the proximal operator \(\text{prox}_{\alpha_1 w}(\mathbf{\cdot})\) which is defined as

(93)¶\[ \text{prox}_{\alpha_1 w}(\mathbf{z}) = \arg\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{PL}}\left\{ \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 + \alpha_1 \mathcal{R}_3(\mathbf{y}) + \delta_{\geq0}(\mathbf{y}) \right\},\]

where \(\alpha_1>0\) is the stepsize and we set it as the reciprocal of the largest eigenvalue of \(\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}\). To obtain the proximal operator, we resort to the dual problem of the minimization in Eq. (93) instead [172] and establish the solution to the dual problem of Eq. (93) as

(94)¶\[\label{eq:prox-dual} \bar{\mathbf{u}} \in \text{arg}\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2NL+3PL}} \left\{\frac{1}{2}\| \mathbf{z} - \mathbf{L}^T \mathbf{u} \|^2_2\right\}, \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \|\mathbf{u}\|_{\infty} \le \alpha_1 \text{ and } \mathbf{L}^T\mathbf{u}<\mathbf{z}.\]

We solve the dual problem using accelerated gradient descent (another FISTA, similar to the approach in [173]) followed by a projection onto the \(L_{\infty}\)-ball during optimization, where the projection operator \(\text{proj}_{\alpha}(\cdot)\) applies element-wise to a vector \(\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^N\) and is defined as

(95)¶\[\begin{split} \text{proj}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})_n = \left\{ \begin{aligned} & x_n, \quad |x_n| \leq \alpha \\ & \alpha, \quad x_n > \alpha \\ & -\alpha, \quad x_n < -\alpha \end{aligned} \right. \quad n=1, \ldots, N.\end{split}\]

Finally, we retrieve the proximal operator in Eq. (93) via \(\text{prox}_{\alpha w}(\mathbf{z})=\max(\mathbf{z} - \mathbf{L}^T\bar{\mathbf{u}}, \mathbf{0})\) (See Algorithm 4).

Algorithm 3 (Proximal Gradient Algorithm for the Main Problem)

Input: initial guess \(\mathbf{c}_0=\mathbf{0}\), \(\boldsymbol{\xi}_0=\mathbf{0}\), the maximal number of iterations \(K_1\), stopping thresholds \(\varepsilon_1>0\), and \(\varepsilon_2>0\)

  1. Set \(k=0\), \(t_0=1\), and cost \(f_0=\infty\)

  2. Compute stepsize \(\alpha=1/\text{eig}_{\max}(\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A})\)

  3. While \(k \le K_1\):

    1. \(\mathbf{c}_{k+1} = \text{prox}_{\alpha w} \left(\ \boldsymbol{\xi}_k - \alpha \left(\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A} \boldsymbol{\xi}_k - \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{p}\right)\right)\)

    2. \(t_{k+1} = \frac{1+\sqrt{4 t_k^2 + 1}}{2}\)

    3. \(\boldsymbol{\xi}_k = \mathbf{c}_{k+1} + \frac{t_k - 1}{t_k + 1}(\mathbf{c}_{k+1} - \mathbf{c}_k)\)

    4. If \(k>1\) and \(\frac{|f_k - f_{k-1}|}{|f_{k-1}|} < \varepsilon_1\) or \(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k} - \boldsymbol{\xi}_{k-1}\|_2}{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k-1}\|_2} < \varepsilon_2\):

      break

    5. \(k \leftarrow k+1\)

Output \(\mathbf{c}_{k+1}\)

Algorithm 4 (Computation of the Proximal Operator)

Input initial guess \(\mathbf{u}_0=\mathbf{0}\), \(\boldsymbol{\eta}_0=\mathbf{0}\) , and the maximal number of iterations \(K_2\)

  1. Set \(t_0=1\)

  2. Compute stepsize \(\beta=1/\text{eig}_{\max}(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T)\)

  3. For \(k=0:K_2\):

    1. \(\mathbf{u}_{k+1} = \text{proj}_{\alpha_1} \left(\ \boldsymbol{\eta}_k - \alpha_2 \left(\mathbf{L} \mathbf{L}^T \boldsymbol{\eta}_k - \mathbf{L} \mathbf{z}\right) \right)\)

    2. \(t_{k+1} = \frac{1+\sqrt{4 t_k^2 + 1}}{2}\)

    3. \(\boldsymbol{\eta}_k = \mathbf{u}_{k+1} + \frac{t_k - 1}{t_k + 1}(\mathbf{u}_{k+1} - \mathbf{u}_k)\)

Output \(\max(\mathbf{z} - \mathbf{L}^T \mathbf{u}_{k+1}, \mathbf{0})\)
