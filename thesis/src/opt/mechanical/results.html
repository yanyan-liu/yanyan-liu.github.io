Results¶

Phantom Results¶

To validate our calibration method, we simulated a 3D cube of size \(512^3\) pixels that contains 150 fluorescent beads at random locations as ground truth. A positive constant accumulative error of 0.05 degrees in the rotation angles and a constant shift of 8 pixels along \(x\) axis were added to the forward model to synthesize a set of 300 projections as measurements. Then, we downsampled the measurements to a coarse scale of (\(128\times128\times300\)) pixels, using the MATLAB function \(\texttt{imresize}\) with bicubic interpolation, for calibration. Choice of initial guess was described in Multiscale Joint Reconstruction-Calibration Algorithm. Reconstruction step of the algorithm was implemented using GlobalBioIm, an open-source library that provides easy-to-use computational modules for solving inverse problems in computational bio-imaging [82]. Calibration step employed functions from the Cryo-Em-Refinement library [87].

In total, we adopted 10 global rounds of joint reconstruction-calibration, each composed of 30 iterations of reconstruction and 6 iterations of calibration to avoid overfitting in the presence of model mismatch. After having obtained the calibrated system parameters, we reconstructed the 3D volume at fine scale (\(512^3\)). We show that without any calibration or shift correction, reconstruction contains both circle and seagull-shaped artifacts (Fig. 13 (a)); with only shift correction, reconstruction still suffers from seagull artifacts (Fig. 13 (b)). After applying our calibration algorithm, we successfully remove both circle and seagull artifacts (Fig. 13 (c)).

Experimental Results¶

We follow steps in the flowchart (Fig. 12) to further validate our algorithm on experimental datasets of a fluorescent textile fiber with controlled in-plane tilt angles of -3.5, 0, 2.5, and 4 degrees. Data were acquired using a focal-sheet-scanning OPT system [114] which employs a lateral light-sheet illumination to reduce the out-of-focus blur in the images and enables the assumption of straight-ray projections. We used 720 projections of size \((256 \times 256)\) that we downsampled to a coarse scale of \((64 \times 64 \times 720)\) pixels. We used 6 global rounds of joint reconstruction-calibration, each composed of 30 iterations of reconstruction and 5 iterations of calibration.

We visualize 3D reconstructions of the fiber with a four-degree tilt in Fig. 14. Without any correction, we see multiple ghost shadows (Fig. 14 (a)). After shift correction, these shadows are partially reduced (Fig. 14 (b)). After applying our calibration algorithm, we effectively reduce these artifacts (Fig. 14 (b)). We show evolution of the cost during the joint reconstruction-calibration in Fig. 15(a). The cost is reduced after each calibration step, especially during the first three rounds, yielding a 73% decrease of cost after 6 global rounds, compared to the reconstruction-only scenario without calibration. This confirms that calibration managed to reduce the model mismatch.

Finally, we compare the four tilt angles recovered by the calibration algorithm and their controlled counterparts in Fig. 15 (b), which show successful outcomes.

The code and data to produce the results are available online [117].
