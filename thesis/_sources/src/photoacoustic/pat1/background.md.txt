(pat1-background)=
# Background

As mentioned in the previous section, photo-switching proteins have a special photo-physical property of undergoing
a change in their extinction spectrum when illuminated by light of ON and OFF wavelengths.
This switching is reversible and robust to photobleaching {cite:p}`chee_vivo_2018, mishra_multiplexed_2020`.
Combined with tailored illumination schedules, it can greatly improve imaging specificity:
weak modulated signals generated by photo-switching targets can be extracted from
strong constant signals from tissue background {cite:p}`stiel_high-contrast_2015, dean-ben_light_2015,
yao_multiscale_2016, li_small_2018, mishra_multiplexed_2020, mark_dual-wavelength_2018, chen_reversibly_2024`.
Photo-switching protein reporters combined with PA tomography (PAT) has demonstrated great potential in
high-specificity multi-label imaging
{cite:p}`chee_vivo_2018, mishra_multiplexed_2020, li_small_2018, liu_model-based_2025`.

(pat1-principles-of-photo-switching-pat)=
## Principles of Photo-Switching PAT

During a photo-switching PAT experiment, {math}`N^{\textrm{ON}}_\textrm{pulse}` laser pulses at wavelength
{math}`\lambda_{\textrm{ON}}` are first sent onto the sample until the proteins are switched to their ON state.
Then, a sequence of {math}`N^{\textrm{OFF}}_{\textrm{pulse}}` pulses
{math}`\lambda_{\textrm{OFF}}` progressively switches the proteins to their OFF state.
This sequence results in a decay of the PA signal from photo-switching reporters that is well described by an
exponential model.
This alternation of ON and OFF cycles, also called a switching cycle, is repeated {math}`N_{\textrm{cycle}}` times.
Background signal remains unmodulated during this process.
The principle of photo-switching PAT is summarized in {numref}`fig:pat1-principle`.

In principle, time series of either ON- or OFF- switching cycles can be used for unmixing.
However, OFF-switching signals are often preferred because they are slower, red-shifted, and have a higher dynamic
range for all photoswitchable proteins used so far {cite:p}`stankevych_practical_2021`.
These properties ease data analysis and allow for larger imaging depth.

## State of the Art

Temporal evolution of the intensity of PA signals at each spatial location approximately follows a decaying
exponential model {cite:p}`stiel_high-contrast_2015, stankevych_practical_2021, dean-ben_light_2015, chee_vivo_2018,
liu_model-based_2025`.
The speed of OFF-switching, indicated by the exponent parameter, is key to distinguishing
different species and the background.
Several pixelwise techniques have been developed to analyze OFF-switching time series of PA images.
Differential imaging was one of the first methods proposed {cite:p}`yao_multiscale_2016`.
It is based on a subtraction between the fully switched OFF- and ON- images (i.e. the last frame of each cycle),
but can only account for a single protein species.
Recent studies have proposed methods to unmix several species by analyzing
the full time series, for example, via exponential fitting and Fourier analysis
{cite:p}`mishra_multiplexed_2020`, or using a specific labeling method with pure and mixed species of two proteins
{cite:p}`li_small_2018`, or employing reporters with non-overlapping spectra {cite:p}`chee_vivo_2018`.
Yet these methods cannot extend to more than two types of labels, or they require strict non-overlapping constraints.
A precise reconstruction based on a detailed description of the underlying physics of photo-switching would be
the most informative and robust to noise.
It requires a good characterization of the generation of switching PA signals which depends on the intrinsic
kinetic constants of proteins and the intensity of local light fluence.

## Contribution

We propose a reconstruction framework for temporal unmixing of photo-switching PAT.
The foundation of our method is a physical model that describes the temporal multiplexing of PA signals during the
photo-switching process.
This method operates on entire images instead of pixelwise, which enables us to leverage powerful and flexible
regularization techniques to improve the unmixing quality and produce coherent intensity maps.
We show on both simulated and experimental phantoms that we can faithfully recover concentration maps of multiple
overlapping protein species, after we have estimated light fluence distribution inside the sample via
diffusion equation.
We further demonstrate on a mouse model that our method successfully unmixes three species based
on minimal assumptions on the physical model.
