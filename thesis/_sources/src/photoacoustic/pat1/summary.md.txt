# Summary

We presented a model-based unmixing framework to reconstruct concentration maps of multiple species of
photo-switching reporters using photoacoustic tomography.
This model describes how time series of PA images evolve during the OFF-switching process.
Our model explicitly relates unknown concentration maps to the factors that govern the switching dynamics, namely,
reporter kinetic constant, extinction coefficient, and distribution of light fluence in the sample.
We make the best use of full time-series information during OFF-switching cycles on the whole image, thus avoiding
shortcomings of pixelwise analysis.
This also enables the addition of carefully designed spatial regularization to the inverse reconstruction problem.
The combination of total variation and {math}`\ell_1` regularizers helps reduce noise and improves the separation of
different species of reporters, which is crucial to a successful unmixing and high-quality reconstruction.

The proposed framework offers fast and efficient quantitative analysis of temporally entangled PA data during
photo-switching experiments.
This approach is versatile and easily applicable to various geometries such as 2D cross-sectioning or whole 3D volumes
acquired with a raster-scanning or tomographic PA setup.
Our framework also allows for a realistic heterogeneous light fluence distribution map, which can be separately
estimated with another model, to be conveniently incorporated in the forward model for high-quality volume unmixing.
