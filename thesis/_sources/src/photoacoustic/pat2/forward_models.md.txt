(pat2-forward-pipeline)=
# Forward Models: from Concentration Maps to Acoustic Signals

(pat2-photo-switching-model)=
## Photo-Switching Model

We carry out our modeling on a 2D sample during one OFF-switching cycle.
We define the sample to be imaged as a function of spatial locations {math}`\mathbf{r}=(x, z)\in\mathbb{R}^2`
with a compact support {math}`\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^2`.
The forward model of photo-switching has been previously derived in section [](#pat1-modeling), and
we briefly recall it here
```{math}
:label: eq:final-H-pat2
    H(\mathbf{r}, t) = 
    \Phi(\mathbf{r})\left(\sum_{p=1}^{P}
    \left(\overline{\varepsilon}_p
    \text{e}^{-k_p\Phi(\mathbf{r})t} +
    \varepsilon_p^{\text{OFF}}\right)
    c_p(\mathbf{r})\right),
```
where {math}`c_p(\mathbf{r})` is the spatial distribution map of the {math}`p`th species and the unknown quantity that
we are interested in.
The non-switching background is included in {eq}`eq:final-H-pat2` as the {math}`P`th reporter with {math}`k_P=0` and
{math}`\overline{\varepsilon}_P=0`.

(pat2-optical-model)=
## Optical Model

We precompute the fluence map {math}`\Phi^0(\mathbf{r})` without the contribution of the reporters
and assume that {math}`\Phi(\mathbf{r}) \approx \Phi^0(\mathbf{r})`, similar to the case for PAT.
Illumination is wide-field and can be assumed homogeneous when it reaches the sample surface.
We model it with a function {math}`S(\mathbf{r})`
that describes a line segment with center {math}`\mathbf{r}_c = (x_c, 0)`, of length {math}`W>0` and
uniform intensity {math}`I_0>0`
```{math}
    :label: eq:light-source-pat2
    S(\mathbf{r}) = \left\{
        \begin{array}{ll}
        I_0, & \text{if } |x-x_c|<\frac{W}{2}\\
        0, & \text{else}.
        \end{array}
    \right.
```

We set the absorption coefficient map {math}`\mu_a(\mathbf{r})` and scattering coefficient map {math}`\mu_s(\mathbf{r})`
the same as the counterparts of the nonswitching background because we assume the contribution from reporters
to be negligible.
The reduced scattering coefficient {math}`\mu_s'(\mathbf{r})` is defined as
```{math}
:label: eq:def-of-mu_s'
\mu_s'(\mathbf{r}) = (1-g)\mu_s(\mathbf{r}),
```
where the anisotropy factor {math}`g\in(0, 1)` is set to a typical value of {math}`0.9`.
Following a similar approach to section [](#pat-compute-fluence) in the previous part, we solve the pair of equations
{eq}`eq:de` and {eq}`eq:bc` combined with the illumination in {eq}`eq:light-source-pat2`,
to obtain a map of the fluence distribution within the sample which we use to construct the forward model
{eq}`eq:final-H-pat2` for photo-switching PAM.

(pat2-pa-effect)=
## Photoacoustic Effect

The deposited optical energy {math}`H` absorbed by the tissue leads to a local rise in temperature.
It causes a thermo-elastic expansion of the tissue and produces acoustic waves that propagate through the sample.
The detector on the sample surface records acoustic waves as measurements.

Because acoustic propagation and detection is independent for each pulse and its mathematical model takes the
same form, we there from omit the notation {math}`t` in the modeling of a single acoustic process for simplicity.
The generated initial pressure (acoustic) field {math}`b(\mathbf{r})` is proportional to the optical energy such that
```{math}
:label: eq:pa-effect
    b(\mathbf{r}) = \iota(\mathbf{r}) H(\mathbf{r}),
```
where {math}`\iota(\mathbf{r})` is the unitless Grueneisen coefficient.
It indicates the efficiency of conversion between heat and pressure,
and we assume it to be constant and set its value to 1 for simplicity
{cite:p}`haltmeier_single-stage_2015, javaherian_direct_2019`.

(pat2-acoustic-model)=
## Acoustic Model


During one acoustic process, ultrasonic waves originating from sources within the sample propagate through
the sample and are detected by the transducer array on the sample surface.
We assume a constant speed of sound {math}`c_0` and an acoustically homogeneous and non-attenuating medium.
We denote by {math}`\tau` the acoustic propagation time (microsecond scale, propagation of sound over a few millimeters)
to distinguish it from the switching time {math}`t` (millisecond scale, repetition rate of the laser).

Following the principles of SAFT, we assume
that signal from the focal point arrives at the same time on all the area of the transducer element,
owing to its parabolic geometry,
Therefore, we use the focal spot as a reference and propagate back to it.
We denote the transducer position by {math}`\mathbf{r}_{\text{T}}=(x_{\text{T}}, 0)`,
the focal length by a constant {math}`f>0`, and location of the focal spot by
{math}`\mathbf{r}_{\text{F}}=(x_{\text{T}}, z_{\text{F}})`.
The detected signal {math}`q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)` on the transducer at time {math}`\tau`,
comes from the contributions of all the point sources
lying on the (upper) semicircle
{math}`C_1^{(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)} = \left\{ \mathbf{r} : |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{F}}| =
f - c_0\tau, z<z_{\text{F}} \right\}` centered at
{math}`\mathbf{r}_{\text{F}}` with radius {math}`(f-c_0\tau)` when {math}`c_0\tau < f`,
and on another (lower) semicircle
{math}`C_2^{(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)} = \left\{ \mathbf{r} : |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{F}}| =
c_0\tau - f, z>z_{\text{F}} \right\}` when {math}`c_0\tau > f`.
At the focal spot {math}`\mathbf{r}_\text{F}`, i.e., when {math}`c_0\tau=f`,
{math}`q` is defined directly as {math}`b(\mathbf{r}_\text{F})`.
Further, we take into account the influence of the sensitivity of the transducer,
described by its (shifted) spatial response function {math}`h(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{T}})`
at the location {math}`\mathbf{r}` of the point source (see {numref}`fig:acoustic-signal-generation` (a)).
Hence, we integrate the weighted amplitude {math}`b(\mathbf{r})h(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{T}})`
on {math}`C_1^{(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)}` when {math}`c_0\tau < f` as
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-1d
    q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau) = \int_{C_1^{(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)}}
    b(\mathbf{r})h(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{T}}) \text{d}s,
```
and on {math}`C_2^{(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)}` when {math}`c_0\tau > f` as
```{math}
:label: eq:q-below-1d
    q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau) = \int_{C_2^{(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)}}
    b(\mathbf{r})h(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{T}}) \text{d}s,
```
where {math}`\text{d}s` is the 1D arc-length element.
We denote {math}`r_\tau=f-c_0\tau` for the sake of simplicity.

By representing the 1D line integral as a constrained 2D integral via a Dirac delta function,
we formulate  {eq}`eq:q-above-1d` equivalently as
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-2d
    q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau) = 
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}x \int_{-\infty}^{z_{\text{F}}}\text{d}z \;
    b(x, z)  h(x - x_{\text{T}}, z - z_{\text{F}})
    \delta\left((x - x_{\text{T}})^2 + (z - z_{\text{F}})^2 - r_\tau^2 \right) (2r_\tau),
```
where {math}`2r_\tau` represents the Jacobian correction to account for the change of variables in the Dirac delta function.
Similarly, for  {eq}`eq:q-below-1d`, we obtain
```{math}
    :label: eq:q-below-2d
    q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau) = 
    \int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}x \int_{z_{\text{F}}}^{\infty}\text{d}z \;
    b(x, z) h(x - x_{\text{T}}, z - z_{\text{F}})
    \delta\left((x - x_{\text{T}})^2 + (z - z_{\text{F}})^2 - r_\tau^2 \right) (2r_\tau).
```

Next, we take into account the electrical impulse response {math}`v(\tau)` of the transducer
and model its effect as the temporal convolution of {math}`q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)` with {math}`v(\tau)`
```{math}
    :label: eq:acoustic-temporal-conv
    p(x_{\text{T}}, \tau) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau')  v(\tau - \tau') \text{d}\tau',
```
where {math}`p(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)` is the noise-free acoustic signal detected on the transducer.

(pat2-pull-forward)=
## Full Forward

Putting every piece of the puzzle together, we have the full forward process of photo-switching PAM
as shown in {numref}`fig:pat2-full-pipeline`.

```{figure} images/full_pipeline.*
:name: fig:pat2-full-pipeline
:width: 100%

Forward pipeline and inversion approaches.
Solid and dashed arrows represent forward and inversion processes, respectively.
```
This pipeline takes concentration maps as input, enters them in the multiplexing module where
the optical process is applied {math}`N` times for {math}`N` photo-switching pulses
to obtain a stack of {math}`N` PA images.
These images are fed to the acoustic module where an acoustic detection process is applied to each of them.
The final output is a stack of acoustic images (measurements).
The goal of unmixing is to recover unknown concentration maps.

There are two ways to accomplish this (see {numref}`fig:pat2-full-pipeline`).
A commonly adopted approach is two-step unmixing.
One first reconstructs the PA images by solving an inverse problem involving the acoustic process;
then, one apply an unmixing algorithm on them.
An alternative approach is to solve a global inverse problem that recovers concentration maps
directly from acoustic measurements.
These two approaches will be detailed in [](#pat2-inverse-problem).

(pat2-numerical-implementation)=
# Numerical Implementation

We discretize the 2D object domain {math}`\Omega` into a collection of points
{math}`\Delta\boldsymbol{i} = (\Delta_1 i_1, \Delta_2 i_2)`,
where {math}`\boldsymbol{i} = (i_1, i_2) \in \Omega_{\text{2D}}\subset \mathbb{Z}^2` is the index of sampled points,
{math}`i_1 = 0, \ldots, L_\text{x} - 1`, and {math}`i_2=0, \ldots, L_\text{z} - 1`.
The diagonal matrix {math}`\Delta = \text{diag}\left\{\Delta_1, \Delta_2\right\}`
defines the stepsize {math}`\Delta_1` and {math}`\Delta_2` for the lateral and axial direction, respectively.
Switching time {math}`t` is sampled at {math}`N` pulses such that {math}`t_n = \Delta_{\text{t}} n`,
{math}`n=0, \ldots, N-1`, with {math}`\Delta_{\text{t}}` the temporal stepsize which is determined
by the laser repetition rate during switching.

(pat2-discrete-photo-switching-model)=
## Discrete Photo-Switching Model

The discretization of {eq}`eq:final-H-pat2` follows the same procedure as in [](#pat1-forward-model-discrete).
We briefly recall the equation here for completeness:
```{math}
\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{S}\mathbf{c},
```
with the deposited energy vector {math}`\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{NL}`,
concentration maps {math}`\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{PL}`,
and the pooled system matrix {math}`\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{NL \times PL}`.

We compute the fluence map by applying the finite element method to
the diffusion equation {eq}`eq:de` with the boundary condition {eq}`eq:bc` and the light source defined in
{eq}`eq:light-source-pat2` and by following the same methodology described in
[](#pat1-diffusion-equation-implementation).

(pat2-discrete-acoustic-model)=
## Discrete Acoustic Model

Implementation of the acoustic model lies in the discretization of
{eq}`eq:q-above-2d` and {eq}`eq:q-below-2d`, where {math}`q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)`
depends on both space and time.
Here, we introduce a convenient variable {math}`y = c_0\tau` and express {math}`q(x_{\text{T}}, \tau)`
equivalently in pure spatial coordinates as {math}`q(x_{\text{T}}, y)` via a change of variables.
We discretize the measurement domain into a grid of pixels {math}`\Gamma\boldsymbol{m}`,
where {math}`\boldsymbol{m}=(m_1, m_2) \in \Theta_{\text{2D}}\subset\mathbb{Z}_2` is the pixel index,
{math}`m_1=0, \ldots, M_1 - 1`, and {math}`m_2=0, \ldots, M_2 - 1`.
The set of indices {math}`\Theta_{\text{2D}}` has {math}`M = M_1M_2` elements.
The diagonal matrix {math}`\Gamma = \text{diag}(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)`
contains the sampling stepsize {math}`\gamma_1` and {math}`\gamma_2` for the lateral and axial direction, respectively.

We define the forward model described in {eq}`eq:q-above-2d`
as a linear operator {math}`\mathcal{L}:L_2(\mathbb{R}^2)\to L_2(\mathbb{R}^2)`
and write {eq}`eq:q-above-2d` equivalently as
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-compact
    q(x_{\text{T}}, y) = \mathcal{L}\{b\}(x_{\text{T}}, y).
```
Similarly, the discretized measurement function {math}`q:\Theta_{\text{2D}}\to\mathbb{R}` can be written as
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-compact-discrete
    q[\boldsymbol{m}] = \mathcal{L}\{b\}
    (\Gamma\boldsymbol{m}).
```
We represent the compactly supported function {math}`b(\mathbf{r})` (equivalently {math}`H(\mathbf{r})`,
c.f {eq}`eq:pa-effect`) via a series of shifted basis functions {math}`\varphi`
```{math}
:label: eq:b-on-basis
    b(\mathbf{r})
    = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Omega_{\text{2D}}} \beta[\boldsymbol{k}] \varphi(\mathbf{r} / \Delta - \boldsymbol{k}), \quad \mathbf{r}\in \Omega.
```
Here, we choose the basis function {math}`\varphi(\mathbf{r})` to be the 2D rectangular function
{math}`\text{rect}(\mathbf{r})` to represent regular pixel grid, where
```{math}
    \text{rect}(\mathbf{r}) = \left\{
    \begin{aligned}
        1, & \quad -\frac{1}{2}\leq x \leq \frac{1}{2} \text{ and } -\frac{1}{2}\leq z \leq \frac{1}{2}, \\
    0, & \quad \text{ else}.
    \end{aligned}\right.
```
Thanks to the linearity of {math}`\mathcal{L}`, we obtain
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-on-basis
        q[\boldsymbol{m}] = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Omega_{\text{2D}}}\beta[\boldsymbol{k}] \mathcal{L}\{\varphi(\cdot / \Delta -\boldsymbol{k})\}(\Gamma\boldsymbol{m}),
```
where the quantity {math}`\mathcal{L}\{\varphi(\cdot / \Delta -\boldsymbol{k})\}(\Gamma\boldsymbol{m})`
has the expression
```{math}
:label: eq:L-above
     \mathcal{L}\{\varphi(\cdot / \Delta -\boldsymbol{k})\}(\Gamma\boldsymbol{m}) =  \int_{I_{\text{x}}}\int_{I_{\text{z}}} h(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_T) \delta\left((x - x_{\text{T}})^2 + (z - z_{\text{F}})^2 -r_\tau^2 \right) (2r_\tau)\text{d}z\text{d}x,
```
with {math}`I_{\text{x}} = [(k_1 - 1/2)\Delta_1, (k_1 + 1/2)\Delta_1]`,
and {math}`I_{\text{z}} = [(k_2 - 1/2)\Delta_2, (k_2 + 1/2)\Delta_2]`.

To compute a discrete version of this integral,
we express the function {math}`h(\mathbf{r})` on the same rectangular basis with the same grid
and support as {math}`b(\mathbf{r})`:
```{math}
:label: eq:h_T-on-basis
    h(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Omega_{\text{2D}}} \eta[\boldsymbol{k}]
    \text{rect}(\mathbf{r} / \Delta -\boldsymbol{k}), \quad \mathbf{r}\in \Omega.
```
We also assume that the discretization grids {math}`\Omega_{\text{2D}}` and {math}`\Theta_{\text{2D}}` match
and that {math}`\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{T}} = (x_\text{T},  z_\text{F}) = \Delta \boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}`
with {math}`\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}} \in \mathbb{Z}^2` the indices of the transducer focal position.
Then,
```{math}
:label: eq:h_T-shifted-on-basis
    h(\mathbf{r} - \boldsymbol{r}_{\text{T}}) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Omega_{\text{2D}}}
    \eta[\boldsymbol{k} - \boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}] \text{rect}(\mathbf{r} / \Delta -\boldsymbol{k}),
    \quad \mathbf{r}\in \Omega.
```

In {eq}`eq:L-above`, {math}`h` is constant in the support {math}`(I_\text{x} \times I_\text{z})`, such that:
```{math}
:label: eq:L-above2
     \mathcal{L}\{\varphi(\cdot-\boldsymbol{k})\}(\Gamma\boldsymbol{m}) = 
     \eta[\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}_\text{T}]
     \int_{I_{\text{x}}}\int_{I_{\text{z}}}
     \delta\left((x - x_{\text{T}})^2 + (z - z_{\text{F}})^2 - r_\tau^2 \right) (2r_\tau)\text{d}z\text{d}x,
```
The integral in  {eq}`eq:L-above2`
reduces to computing the arc length of the semicircle intersecting with the 2D box defined by
{math}`(I_{\text{x}}\times I_{\text{z}})`, which we denote by {math}`s_{\boldsymbol{m}}[\boldsymbol{k}]`.
Therefore, {eq}`eq:q-above-on-basis` becomes
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-simplified
    q[\boldsymbol{m}] = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Omega_{\text{2D}}} \beta[\boldsymbol{k}]
    \eta[\boldsymbol{k} - \boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}]  s_{\boldsymbol{m}}[\boldsymbol{k}].
```

The precise computation of {math}`s_{\boldsymbol{m}}[\boldsymbol{k}]` for all spatial locations is expensive,
especially for large-scale reconstructions.
For computational speed, we use a unit length of 1 for all pixels that intersect with the semicircle.
 {eq}`eq:q-above-simplified` simplifies to
```{math}
:label: eq:q-above-final
    q[\boldsymbol{m}] = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Lambda_+^{\boldsymbol{m}}}\beta[\boldsymbol{k}]
    \eta[\boldsymbol{k} - \boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}],
```
where we denote by {math}`\Lambda_+^{\boldsymbol{m}}` the discrete upper semicircle.
It is the set of pixel indices {math}`\boldsymbol{k}` whose corresponding pixels intersect with
the continuous upper semicircle {math}`C_+^{\mathbf{r}_{\text{T}, \boldsymbol{m}}}`
```{math}
:label: eq:discrete-upper-circle
     C_+^{\mathbf{r}_{\text{T}, \boldsymbol{m}}}= \left\{ (x, z): (x - x_{\text{T}, m_1})^2 + (z - z_{\text{F}})^2 =
     r_{\tau}^2 \text{ and } z< z_{\text{F}}\right\},
```
where {math}`x_{\text{T}, m_1}` is the location of the transducer on the measurement grid.

Similarly, in the case of the lower semicircle, we have
```{math}
    :label: eq:q-below-final
    q[\boldsymbol{m}] = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in \Lambda_-^{\boldsymbol{m}}}\beta[\boldsymbol{k}]
    \eta[\boldsymbol{k} - \boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}],
```
where {math}`\Lambda_-^{\boldsymbol{m}}` is the discrete lower semicircle whose corresponding pixels intersect with the continuous lower semicircle {math}`C_-^{\mathbf{r}_{\text{T}, \boldsymbol{m}}}`
```{math}
:label: eq:discrete-lower-circle
     C_-^{\mathbf{r}_{\text{T}, \boldsymbol{m}}}= \left\{ (x, z): (x - x_{\text{T}, m_1})^2 + (z - z_{\text{F}})^2 =
     r_{\tau}^2 \text{ and } z> z_{\text{F}}\right\}.
```
In our implementation, {math}`\Lambda_+^{\boldsymbol{m}}` and {math}`\Lambda_-^{\boldsymbol{m}}`
are generated via a function
[`skimage.draw.circle_perimeter`](https://scikit-image.org/docs/0.25.x/api/skimage.draw.html\#skimage.draw.circle\_perimeter)
from the imaging processing library _scikit-image_ {cite}`skimage`.
{eq}`eq:q-above-final` (and similarly, {eq}`eq:q-below-final`) can be implemented based on the following steps:
for each measurement location {math}`\boldsymbol{m}`,

1) Shift {math}`\eta` to grid location of the transducer, then, element-wise multiply {math}`\beta` with it;
2) Generate the semicircle mask {math}`\Lambda_+^{\boldsymbol{m}}` and apply it to the outcome of Step 1);
3) Sum up all masked pixels {math}`\boldsymbol{k}` to obtain {math}`q[\boldsymbol{m}]`.

Since the radius of the semicircle is the same for a given depth, we can generate a row of the image
{math}`q` at once.
To avoid explicitly constructing the forward matrix by computing {eq}`eq:q-above-final`
for each pixel {math}`\boldsymbol{m}`,
we interpret {eq}`eq:q-above-final` equivalently as follows:
the pixel value {math}`q[\boldsymbol{m}]` is the result of the correlation between a 2D image {math}`\beta`
and a 2D image {math}`\eta` multiplied by a binary mask,
then evaluated at location {math}`\boldsymbol{k}_{\text{T}}`.
We thus implement {eq}`eq:q-above-final` (similarly {eq}`eq:q-below-final`) based on the following steps:
1) Generate the semicircle mask at current depth {math}`k_{2}`;
2) Element-wise multiply it with the SPR image (see {numref}`fig:acoustic-signal-generation` (c) and (c'));
3) Correlate the PA image {math}`\beta` with the masked SPR image;
4) Extract the horizontal slice at focal plane {math}`k_{\text{T}, 2}` from the outcome of Step 3);
5) Perform Step 1) to 4) for all depths and stack the horizontal slices according to their respective location to
produce the 2D image {math}`q`.
The temporal convolution step is applied to each column of the 2D image {math}`q` via standard convolution in 1D.
We provide an illustration of the acoustic signal-generation process in {numref}`fig:acoustic-signal-generation`.

We write the forward model for a single acoustic process at switching time point {math}`t_n` as the linear system
```{math}
    :label: eq:acoustic-discrete-single
    \mathbf{p}^n = \mathbf{W} \mathbf{b}^n,
```
where {math}`\mathbf{b}^n` and {math}`\mathbf{p}^n \in\mathbb{R}^{L}` are the vector representation
of the PA image {math}`\beta` and the acoustic image {math}`p` at {math}`t_n`, respectively.
We construct the acoustic forward operator {math}`\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{L \times L}` as a matrix-free linear
operator following the interpretation in {numref}`fig:acoustic-signal-generation`.

Finally, we build the acoustic forward operator {math}`\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}`
for {math}`N` switching pulses during an OFF-switching cycle and obtain
```{math}
:label: eq:acoustic-full-forward

    \underbrace{
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{p}^{0} \\
        \mathbf{p}^{1} \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf{p}^{N-1}
    \end{bmatrix}
    }_{\mathbf{p}}
    =
    \underbrace{
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{W} & 
        \mathbf{0} &     
        \cdots & 
        \mathbf{0} \\
        \mathbf{0} & 
        \mathbf{W} &
        \cdots & 
        \vdots \\
        \vdots & 
        \cdots &    
        \ddots &
        \mathbf{0} \\
        \mathbf{0} & 
        \cdots & 
        \mathbf{0} &     
        \mathbf{W} \\
    \end{bmatrix}}
    _{\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}}
    \underbrace{
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{b}^{0} \\
        \mathbf{b}^{1} \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf{b}^{N-1}
    \end{bmatrix}}
    _{\mathbf{b}}  + \quad\mathbf{n}.
```
In {eq}`eq:acoustic-full-forward`,
{math}`\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{NL}` is the vector of the initial pressure of all {math}`N` pulses,
{math}`\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}\in\mathbb{R}^{NL\times NL}` is the system matrix,
{math}`\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{NL}` is the vector of the acoustic measurement of all {math}`N` pulses,
and the vector {math}`\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{NL}` represents measurement noise.
Note that both {math}`\mathbf{W}` and {math}`\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}` are matrix-free linear operators.

## Complete Forward Pipeline

We denote the forward operator of the complete pipeline as {math}`\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{NL\times PL}`.
It is a composition of the photo-switching forward operator {math}`\mathbf{S}` and the complete acoustic
forward matrix {math}`\mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}`:
```{math}
:label: eq:full-forward
    \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{tot}}\mathbf{S}.
```

The forward pipeline takes the vector {math}`\mathbf{c}` of concentration maps as input,
applies the photo-switching operator {math}`\mathbf{S}` in which the optical process is applied {math}`N`
times for {math}`N` photo-switching pulses to obtain a stack of {math}`N` PA images {math}`\mathbf{H}`
(or equivalently {math}`\mathbf{b}`, cf. {eq}`eq:pa-effect`).
They are then fed to the acoustic module where the acoustic detection process is applied to each PA image.
The final measurement is a stack of acoustic signals {math}`\mathbf{p}`.
