We design a 2D numerical phantom of size {math}`(150\times150)` px in which we randomly insert three groups of disk
targets of diameter between 7 px and 12 px that represent photo-switching reporters on a heterogeneous
background.
The background image is synthesized similarly as in [](#pat1-result-numerical-phantom).
Each target has homogeneous intensity.
The ratio of intensities between the three groups and the background (maximal) is 2:1:1:5,
mimicking a blood-rich sample where the background signal is stronger than that of the reporters.
Each group has the same kinetic constant as one of the three rsOAPs (see {numref}`tab:pat1-reporters`).
We generate measurements simulating an OFF-switching cycle with 50 PA images of size {math}`(150\times150)` px, with
1% additive white Gaussian noise to mimic to experimental datasets (see {numref}`fig:pat1-decay-compare`).

We test three reconstruction approaches, namely, least-square only (LS), least-square regularized with total variation
on the spatial maps of each reporter (LS+TV), and least-square regularized with TV and additionally {math}`\ell_1` among
the reporters (LS+TV+{math}`\ell_1`).
We compare in {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` the results of using these methods against the ground truth.

In all scenarios, the non-switching background
is well recovered with a perfect structural similarity index (SSIM) of 1.0, though the peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR)
varies among different methods ({numref}`fig:pat1-decay-compare` (d), (h), and (l)).

Without any regularization, unmixing performs well only on the Re (with the largest kinetic constant among three rsOAPs),
achieving an SSIM of 0.88 out of 1 ({numref}`fig:pat1-decay-compare` (a)).
However, the algorithm struggles to separate Dr and Rp.
This is indicated by the presence of reporters belonging to Rp appearing in the map of the Dr
({numref}`fig:pat1-decay-compare` (b)-(c)), leading to an SSIM of only 0.73 for Dr.
In fact, Rp reporters appear in images of the first two rsOAPs (see the zoom-in area in
{numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` (a) and (b)).

After adding TV regularizer, we observe in {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` (e)-(g) that
TV not only reduces noise inside the beads, giving a much smoother intensity
distribution for each of them, but also greatly mitigates the cross-talk issue if we compare
{numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` (b) and (f).
PSNR of each group enjoys an average boost of 7 dB and SSIM of reporters Re and Dr is improved by 7\% on average.

However, there still remains a hint of cross-talk (see for example {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` (f)),
which is solved by adding {math}`\ell_1` regularizer to enforce sparsity at each pixel
({numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` (i)-(j)).
The intensity of each bead is more uniform, increasing
the PSNR of each map by another 2-5 dB (see {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg` (i)-(k)).

To gain more insight on the reconstruction quality, we compare intensity profiles along a representative horizontal
line at the 60th row in {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg-profiles` (q)-(s).
We see that LS+TV+{math}`\ell_1` outperforms LS only and LS+TV in the following aspects:

- Signal is correctly recovered at nearly its 100\% intensity with very little fluctuation inside the bead
(see signals at location 55 px and 140 px in {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg-profiles` (s) for a good example)
- LS+TV+{math}`\ell_1` does not suffer from cross-talk on locations where there should not
be any signal compared to the other two approaches.

Taking signals at location 55 px and 140 px in {numref}`fig:pat1-effect-of-reg-profiles` (q) and (r)
as an example, LS only and LS+TV show relatively prominent peaks at these two locations where signals from
the Rp should not appear at all, while LS+TV+{math}`\ell_1` shows only flat tiny bumps.
These details confirm that our proposed reconstruction method, LS+TV+{math}`\ell_1` is effective and
yields the best reconstruction results.

(pat1-optical-parameters)=
## Optical Parameters

The absorption coefficient {math}`\mu_a` of the background is calculated according to {cite:p}`absorption`
```{math}
:label: eq:mu_a-blood

\mu_a(\lambda) = \ln 10 \cdot
\varepsilon(\lambda) [\text{cm}^{-1 }\cdot \text{mole}^{-1} \cdot \text{L}] \cdot 
\frac{x\% \cdot 150 [\text{g}\cdot\text{L}^{-1}]} 
{64500 [\text{g}\cdot\text{mole}^{-1}]},

```
where {math}`\lambda=770` nm is the OFF-switching wavelength,
{math}`\varepsilon(\lambda) = 1311.88 [\text{cm}^{-1 }\cdot \text{mole}^{-1} \cdot \text{L} =
\text{cm}^{-1 }\cdot \text{M}^{-1}]`
is the molar extinction coefficient of blood at 770 nm, assuming the blood we used in the phantom to be mostly composed
of deoxygenated hemoglobin (Hb),
{math}`x=3` is the percentage of blood used in the phantom,
{math}`150[\text{g}\cdot\text{L}^{-1}]` is the typical mass concentration of hemoglobin within blood,
and {math}`64500 [\text{g}\cdot\text{mole}^{-1}]` is the molecular weight of hemoglobin.
Using these values, the background is determined by {math}`\mu_a^{\text{bg}} =  0.21 [\text{cm}^{-1}]`.

The absorption coefficients of the protein species Re and Dr are calculated via {cite:p}`jacques_optical_2013`

```{math}
:label: eq:mu_a-protein

\mu_a(\lambda) = \ln 10 \cdot \varepsilon(\lambda) [\text{M}^{-1}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}] \cdot c [\text{M}]

```

from their molar concentration {math}`c_{\text{Re}}=4.23\times 10^{-6}` [M],
{math}`c_{\text{Dr}}=3.71\times 10^{-6}` [M] and their respective molar extinction coefficients at 770nm
{math}`\varepsilon_{\text{Re}}(770\text{nm})\approx 0.7\times 10^{5}` {math}`[\text{M}^{-1}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}]`,
{math}`\varepsilon_{\text{Dr}}(770\text{nm})\approx 0.4\times 10^{5}` {math}`[\text{M}^{-1}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}]`.
We thus have {math}`\mu_a^{\text{Re}} = 0.68\text{ cm}^{-1}` and {math}`\mu_a^{\text{Dr}} = 0.34\text{ cm}^{-1}`.

We adopt a typical value of {math}`10\text{ cm}^{-1}` for the reduced scattering coefficient {math}`\mu_s^{'}` of the
background, which is composed of 3\% intralipid as a scatterer.

(pat1-estimation-of-kinetic-constants)=
## Estimation of Kinetic Constants

The kinetic constants {math}`\bar{k}` reported in {numref}`tab:pat1-reporters` are measured experimentally and depend
on the pulse energy and the subsequent unknown light fluence {math}`\Phi_m` during the measurement via
{math}`k = \frac{\bar{k}}{\Phi_m}`, where {math}`k` is the intrinsic constant.
To estimate {math}`\Phi_m`, we first use an empirical method to retrieve the decay rates
{math}`\{b_{\text{Re}}, b_{\text{Dr}}, b_{\text{Rp}}\}` of each species from the experimental dataset.
Then, we determine {math}`\Phi_m` from
```{math}
:label: eq:average

\Phi_m = \sqrt[3]{\frac{\bar{k}_{\text{Re}}}{b_{\text{Re}}}\frac{\bar{k}_{\text{Dr}}}
{b_{\text{Dr}}}\frac{\bar{k}_{\text{Rp}}}{b_{\text{Rp}}}},

```

which is the geometric mean of the estimated results from all three species.
To estimate {math}`\{b_{\text{Re}}, b_{\text{Dr}}, b_{\text{Rp}}\}`, we fit an exponential function
```{math}
:label: eq:exp-fit

y = a\text{e}^{-bt} + c

```
at each pixel to obtain a map of decay rates {math}`b`.
Then, we approximate the OFF-switching rate of each reporter by locating the peaks in the
histogram of the map of {math}`b`.

In {numref}`fig:find-k` (a), we show an example of the fitting result at one location inside each of the three rsOAPs
marked by the three dots in {numref}`fig:find-k` (b) for the dataset of the experimental phantom of beads.
In {numref}`fig:find-k` (c), we show the result of the empirical estimate of kinetic constants.
Three prominent peaks are observed, indicating decay rates of the three species.
We take a value around these peaks as the empirical estimate for {math}`\{b_{\text{Re}}, b_{\text{Dr}}, b_{\text{Rp}}\}`.
Plugging the estimated values of {math}`\{b_{\text{Re}}, b_{\text{Dr}}, b_{\text{Rp}}\}` shown in {numref}`fig:find-k`
and {math}`\{\bar{k}_{\text{Re}}, \bar{k}_{\text{Dr}},\bar{k}_{\text{Rp}}\}` in {numref}`tab:pat1-reporters`
into {eq}`eq:average`, we obtain that {math}`\Phi_m = 0.3`.
