# Results

## Phantom Results

To validate our calibration method,
we simulated a 3D cube of size {math}`512^3` pixels
that contains 150 fluorescent beads at random locations as ground truth.
A positive constant accumulative error of 0.05 degrees in the rotation angles and
a constant shift of 8 pixels along {math}`x` axis were added to the forward model
to synthesize a set of 300 projections as measurements.
Then, we downsampled the measurements to a coarse scale of ({math}`128\times128\times300`) pixels, using
the MATLAB function {math}`\texttt{imresize}` with bicubic interpolation, for calibration.
Choice of initial guess was described in [](#opt-calibration-algorithm).
Reconstruction step of the algorithm was implemented using _GlobalBioIm_,
an open-source library that provides easy-to-use computational modules
for solving inverse problems in computational bio-imaging {cite:p}`Soubies:19`.
Calibration step employed functions from the _Cryo-Em-Refinement_ library {cite:p}`zehni_joint_2020`.

In total, we adopted 10 global rounds of joint reconstruction-calibration,
each composed of 30 iterations of reconstruction and 6 iterations of calibration
to avoid overfitting in the presence of model mismatch.
After having obtained the calibrated system parameters,
we reconstructed the 3D volume at fine scale ({math}`512^3`).
We show that without any calibration or shift correction,
reconstruction contains both circle and seagull-shaped artifacts ({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-simu-result` (a));
with only shift correction, reconstruction still suffers from seagull artifacts
({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-simu-result` (b)).
After applying our calibration algorithm,
we successfully remove both circle and seagull artifacts
({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-simu-result` (c)).

```{figure} images/simu_results.*
:name: fig:opt-calibration-simu-result
:width: 90%

Reconstructions (a)-(c) and ground truth (d) at slice 147 (out of 512 along {math}`z` axis).
(a) Without calibration. 
(b) With shift correction only. 
(c) With our calibration algorithm. Intensities are normalized and saturated for better visualization.
```

## Experimental Results

We follow steps in the flowchart ({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-flowchart`)
to further validate our algorithm on experimental datasets of a fluorescent textile fiber
with controlled in-plane tilt angles of -3.5, 0, 2.5, and 4 degrees.
Data were acquired using a focal-sheet-scanning OPT system {cite:p}`Marelli:21`
which employs a lateral light-sheet illumination to reduce the out-of-focus blur in the images and
enables the assumption of straight-ray projections.
We used 720 projections of size {math}`(256 \times 256)`
that we downsampled to a coarse scale of {math}`(64 \times 64 \times 720)` pixels.
We used 6 global rounds of joint reconstruction-calibration,
each composed of 30 iterations of reconstruction and 5 iterations of calibration.

```{figure} images/fiber4000_FBP-FBPshift-calib6noTVrecon.*
:name: fig:opt-calibration-fiber-result
:width: 80%

Snapshots of 3D visualization of reconstructions of a fluorescent textile fiber. 
(a)-(b) Results via the 2D FBP algorithm in slice by slice without correction (a) and with shift correction only (b).
(c) Result via our calibration algorithm.
Intensities of images are normalized and saturated for better visualization.
```

We visualize 3D reconstructions of the fiber with a four-degree tilt
in {numref}`fig:opt-calibration-fiber-result`.
Without any correction, we see multiple ghost shadows ({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-fiber-result` (a)).
After shift correction, these shadows are partially reduced
({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-fiber-result` (b)).
After applying our calibration algorithm, we effectively reduce these artifacts
({numref}`fig:opt-calibration-fiber-result` (b)).
We show evolution of the cost during the joint reconstruction-calibration in
{numref}`fig:opt-calibration-fiber-cost-evolution`(a).
The cost is reduced after each calibration step, especially during the first three rounds,
yielding a 73\% decrease of cost after 6 global rounds,
compared to the reconstruction-only scenario without calibration.
This confirms that calibration managed to reduce the model mismatch.

Finally, we compare the four tilt angles recovered by the calibration algorithm and their controlled counterparts
in {numref}`fig:opt-calibration-fiber-cost-evolution` (b), which show successful outcomes.
